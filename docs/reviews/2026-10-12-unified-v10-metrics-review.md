## 回复全文

(a) Keep v2 as the headline, but name its scope precisely. It measures word onsets from the first annotated pause through the observation-window end, not speech strictly inside pauses and not pause-caused reactions. V1 can improve merely because a reaction shifts past the pause end while still interrupting resumed speech; v2 closes that timing escape without introducing a reaction-time tolerance. Retain v1 as the narrower diagnostic. "No added reaction-time parameter" is more defensible than unqualified "parameter-free."

Excluding pre-first-pause onsets is justified as a scope restriction, not an exoneration. A word already starting before an annotated pause cannot be a response to that future pause. Conversely, occurring after the pause starts does not establish causation. Keep pre-speaking samples in the denominator; exclude only their pre-pause onsets from this numerator. Describe those onsets as "outside this pause-conditioned observation region," not categorically "unrelated to pauses." Their exclusion would not be defensible for a metric claiming to measure all premature speech.

The validation supports a changed classification, not a causal explanation. V2 adds 55 Nemotron samples and three vad500 samples relative to v1. Those are additional samples with post-first-pause word onsets, not necessarily 55 and three delayed, pause-triggered utterance starts. Continuing speech, new speech during resumed user speech, and speech in trailing silence can also qualify. The supplied aggregate tables do not distinguish these mechanisms; freeze the definition independently of the resulting ranking.

The principal reviewer objection is the all-word policy, not architecture dependence. Identical scoring rules do not make "every early word is a takeover" a neutral conversational definition: the paper explicitly excludes backchannels from takeover. Your rule is defensible as a strict word-timing proxy, but it deliberately penalizes potentially legitimate acknowledgments. Replace the document's categorical "cannot be told apart" with "these annotations do not reliably label backchannels versus aborted turn starts; we therefore count every word onset."

(b) Keep the v2 three-way turn-taking split and the all-sample denominator. It prevents early speech from obtaining valid-response latency credit. Preserve the identity early_start + response + no_response = eligible; no_response means silence, not every unsuccessful trial. For example, vad500 has 18 early starts, 101 responses, and zero silent samples: its unreached p90 is expected despite no_response_rate = 0. Do not rank systems using silence rate alone.

A word spanning the first-pause boundary still escapes the onset headline. Executing the attached function with pause [2,3) and one word spanning [1.5,3.5] produces pause_intrusion=False but intrusion_s=1. This is an onset-versus-occupancy distinction, shared with v1, not evidence that the sample is unobtrusive. Preserve the onset definition, but expose pre-pause speech and carry-in overlap diagnostics; any_speech_rate alone does not identify which post-pause-positive samples also had earlier speech.

pause_intrusion_per_pause is an owned-interval statistic, not a probability that each pause triggered a takeover. One continuing utterance can place words in several owned intervals and increment several pauses. Owned intervals also have unequal lengths, and the final interval includes the remaining window. These properties do not invalidate the statistic on a fixed annotation set, but they preclude interpreting its numerator as independent takeover events. Keep it diagnostic; the existing whole-sample bootstrap appropriately avoids treating pauses within a sample as independent.

Silence and canned responses remain complementary gaming paths. Silence throughout a pause sample scores perfectly; an otherwise silent system emitting one canned word immediately after E qualifies as a fast response. Neither unified timing metrics nor the supplied official turn-taking scorer check whether that word answers the user. Compare pause restraint and valid-response rates jointly under the same system configuration; neither is sufficient alone. A single early acknowledgment followed by an excellent answer is conversely a complete strict-response failure, an intentional consequence requiring explicit documentation.

Eligibility exclusions create a real denominator problem. Semmed excludes two captures with buffer-overflow errors; these are system-dependent failures, not merely externally missing annotations. Its 11/214 intrusion rate describes completed eligible captures, not the entire selected panel. Over all 216 selected samples, the unknown-outcome bounds are 11/216 to 13/216, or 5.09%-6.02%, before sampling uncertainty. Report coverage separately, freeze selected sample IDs before generation, and show common-eligible paired comparisons plus missing-outcome sensitivity. Pairing alone does not remove selection bias. Reason counts overlap and must not be summed as numbers of failed samples.

The implemented latency treatment is infinity imputation, not an estimator using right-censored response times. The code replaces both early starts and silence with infinity. Early starts are terminal failures under the strict definition; silent samples merely have no observed response before their window ends. Infinity is defensible as a scoring convention for valid response within the allowed window, not as evidence that a silent system would never respond. Keep observation horizons fixed across systems and label rates beyond the shortest horizon as lower bounds; dropping short-window samples would reintroduce denominator selection.

Interruption scoring retains an unavoidable continuation loophole under the documented crop. A system can talk through the interruption and receive fast-response credit from speech transcribed after R. The existing non-ranking warning is necessary. Change "scores as an instant response" to "can receive near-zero latency credit": cropping does not guarantee that the first recognized word starts exactly at R. Stopping behavior cannot be recovered reliably from the supplied post-R transcript alone.

(c) The "same curve" quantile claim is false for the implemented interpolation. The attached censored_quantile([0.1, 0.2, inf, inf], 0.5) returns infinity, although half the samples have responded by 0.2 seconds. Likewise, nine finite values followed by infinity produce an unreached p90 despite 90% observed responses. To make quantiles genuinely invert the response-rate curve, use Q(q) = inf{t : F(t) >= q}, equivalent to the ceil(q*N)-th ordered value, with failures retained at infinity. Bootstrap that same estimator. Alternatively retain interpolation but remove the exact curve-inversion interpretation.

The scorer does not enforce the documented observation window. pause_sample receives no T and gives the final pause ownership through infinity. A word at 12 seconds therefore counts with T=10. Similarly, turn_taking_sample([5], 1, 2) accepts a response outside its window; interruption scoring can accept negative latency, and response thresholds test only latency <= k. Add explicit timestamp/reference/annotation validation, including nonempty and nonduplicated pause intervals, or establish a tested upstream contract. Hash agreement verifies file consistency, not timestamp validity. These are executable counterexamples, not claims that the supplied captures contain malformed timestamps.

The requested v1 diagnostic is absent from the current implementation. pause_sample emits only the v2 onset quantities, pause-overlap duration, and any-speech flag; the aggregate metrics likewise contain no inside-pause onset rate. Add a separately named row flag and sample-level rate using any(a <= word_start < b). Do not substitute intrusion_s_per_pause_s: a carry-in word can have positive overlap without any inside-pause onset, while a zero-length word can have an onset without positive overlap.

full_duplex_metrics.md:23 presents an assumption as an established boundary. Replace "one user turn that ends with the window" with "we operationally treat the window as a non-yielding user turn." Line 84 already acknowledges that the actual speech end is not measured and trailing-silence replies count as intrusions. The official pause evaluator's whole-output threshold does not establish the true turn end: it reads output chunks, not a user-end annotation. Thus v2 cannot establish that every counted onset was genuinely premature.

full_duplex_metrics.md:10 needs two corrections. A one- or two-word output can count officially when its transcript span is at least one second; the ignored case requires both a span below one second and at most three chunks across the evaluated output. Also, "started speaking ... in 67" should read "contained a word onset at or after the first annotated pause in 67": v2 does not require the utterance's first word to occur there.

full_duplex_metrics.md:32 overstates the duration interpretation. A word starting before the first pause can contribute to intrusion_s_per_pause_s, so it does not necessarily count only in any_speech_rate. Speech beginning after one pause ends can overlap a later pause, so it does not necessarily "add nothing." Define this quantity as coverage of annotated pause time by ASR word spans, not complete acoustic silence occupancy; word spans and zero-length null-end words are the actual inputs. intrusion_words measures word count, not utterance count or duration.

Clarify the denominator and output promises. At line 63, "eligible samples" applies to sample proportions and latency quantiles; the pooled metrics instead divide by pause count and unioned pause seconds from those samples. At lines 74-77, specify that annotation/hash checks cover eligible rows, latency intervals cover the median rather than p90, and ineligible rows retain identifying metadata plus reasons, not literally reasons alone.

Qualify the ASR and reference claims. Microsecond rounding removes floating-point boundary noise, not ASR alignment uncertainty. A shared checkpoint does not establish equal recognition error across systems; a missed or invented word can change affected metrics, rather than necessarily moving every metric as line 86 states. Preserve the validation table's explicit judge qualification when reporting "official" interruption ratings: the supplied result uses a local Qwen3.8-27B judge, so it is a configured reference-evaluator result, not an interchangeable published benchmark score.
